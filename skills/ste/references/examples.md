# Worked examples

Use these as a model when rewriting existing text or when a rule in SKILL.md is unclear in
practice. Each pair shows the non-STE original and the STE rewrite, with a short note on
which rules applied.

## Procedural examples

**Original:** "You should ensure that the power supply has been disconnected prior to
opening the access panel, and then you can carry out an inspection of the wiring for any
discrepancies."

**STE:** "Before you open the access panel, disconnect the power supply. Inspect the
wiring. Look for differences from the wiring diagram."

Notes: split into three sentences (one instruction each); condition moved before the
command; "should ensure" → dropped hedge, made it a direct instruction; "has been
disconnected" (present perfect passive) → imperative "disconnect"; "carry out an
inspection of" → "inspect"; "discrepancies" → "differences."

---

**Original:** "The technician needs to utilize the calibrated torque wrench to tighten the
bolts to the specified value, making sure not to over-tighten them as this could cause
damage to the housing."

**STE:** "Use the calibrated torque wrench. Tighten the bolts to the specified value. Do
not tighten the bolts more than the specified value. Too much force can cause damage to the
housing."

Notes: one action per sentence; "utilize" → "use"; imperative instead of "the technician
needs to"; "could cause" → "can cause" (can = possibility, not could); "over-tighten"
decomposed into a plain instruction plus a stated consequence.

---

**Original:** "If the indicator light is flashing, this could mean that there is a problem
with the sensor, so you might want to replace it."

**STE:** "If the indicator light flashes, the sensor can have a failure. Replace the
sensor."

Notes: condition before command retained; "is flashing" (continuous) → simple present
"flashes"; "could mean," "might want to" → removed hedges, stated as a fact ("can have a
failure") followed by a direct instruction.

## Descriptive examples

**Original:** "The system has been designed to automatically detect anomalies in the data
stream, and it will continuously monitor for any deviations that could potentially indicate
a fault condition."

**STE:** "The system detects unusual data automatically. The system monitors the data
stream at all times. Unusual data can show a fault."

Notes: "has been designed to" (present perfect passive) → simple present, active;
"anomalies" → "unusual data" (concrete, no jargon); "continuously" retained as a plain
adverb but reworded as "at all times" for clarity; "could potentially indicate" → "can
show"; three short sentences instead of one long compound sentence; stayed under the
25-word descriptive limit.

---

**Original:** "Utilization of the backup generator is recommended in the event of a power
outage, since the primary system's functionality may be compromised."

**STE:** "If a power outage occurs, use the backup generator. During a power outage, the
primary system can fail."

Notes: "utilization of ... is recommended" → direct instruction "use"; "in the event that" →
"if"; "functionality may be compromised" → "can fail" (concrete, active, no hedge).

## Warnings and cautions

**Wrong order (risk stated first):** "There is a risk of electric shock from the exposed
terminal, so do not touch it."

**STE:** "WARNING: Do not touch the exposed terminal. The terminal has a dangerous
voltage."

Notes: command comes first, then the risk, per the warning rule in SKILL.md.

---

**Wrong order:** "Improper storage of the reagent can cause it to degrade, so store it
below 8°C."

**STE:** "CAUTION: Store the reagent below 8°C. If you do not store the reagent below 8°C,
the reagent can degrade."

Notes: CAUTION used because the risk is to material (the reagent), not to a person;
condition-then-consequence phrasing after the command.

---

**Note example (information only, no instruction):** "NOTE: The calibration value shown on
the display is an average of the last ten readings, not a single instantaneous
measurement."

Notes: a NOTE never gives an instruction; this one only explains what a value means; it is
under the 25-word descriptive limit.

## Common mistakes and fixes

| Mistake | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "The valve should be closed before you proceed." | Passive voice in a procedural sentence; "should" is a hedge | "Close the valve before you continue." |
| "We have completed the testing of the unit." | Present perfect | "We completed the test of the unit." or "We tested the unit." |
| "Ensure the settings are correct." | Missing "that"; ambiguous "ensure" | "Make sure that the settings are correct." |
| "The operating and maintenance manual" | Noun cluster with an -ing word acting as an adjective, unclear grouping | "the manual for operation and maintenance" |
| "This may cause overheating of the engine, which could lead to failure." | "may," "could," noun "overheating" instead of verb, semicolon-style comma splice | "This can cause the engine to overheat. An overheat can cause a failure." |
| "Remove the cover and check the fuse, then replace it if necessary." | Multiple instructions in one sentence, vague "it," vague "if necessary" | "Remove the cover. Check the fuse. If the fuse is broken, replace the fuse." |
| "The system was calibrated by the technician." | Passive voice with a known, specific agent — passive is not needed here | "The technician calibrated the system." |
| "Unit malfunctions can occur if the input voltage exceeds tolerance; contact support." | Semicolon, noun "malfunctions" instead of verb, vague "tolerance" | "The unit can fail if the input voltage is more than the specified limit. If the unit fails, contact support." |

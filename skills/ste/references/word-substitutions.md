# Word substitutions and one-meaning rulings

This is a working dictionary, not the official ASD-STE100 dictionary. It paraphrases the
kind of substitution ASD-STE100 makes: pick one common, concrete word per meaning and use
it every time, instead of rotating among synonyms. Read this before drafting. It does not
override a domain-specific technical noun or verb (see SKILL.md) — those stay as-is.

## How to use this list

Each row gives an unapproved word or phrase, the approved replacement, and the meaning it
covers. If a word has more than one common meaning, it gets more than one row — use the
approved word only for that meaning, and pick a different approved word for the other
meaning. Do not use the unapproved word for any meaning, even one not listed here; replace
it with the nearest approved word on the list.

## One-word-one-meaning rulings for common ambiguous pairs

- **"close"** (verb, shut) vs. **"close"** (adjective, near): keep "close" only as the verb
  meaning "shut." For nearness, use "near."
- **"check"** (verb, examine) vs. **"check"** (noun, a mark ✓): use "check" only as the
  verb. For the mark, use "checkmark."
- **"light"** (noun, a lamp or indicator) vs. **"light"** (adjective, not heavy): keep both,
  but never use "light" as a verb meaning "ignite" — use "start" or the specific technical
  verb instead.
- **"set"** (verb, adjust to a value) vs. **"set"** (noun, a group of items): keep "set" as
  the verb meaning "adjust." For the group, use "group" or the specific technical noun.

## Adding to this list

When you hit a word during drafting that is not on this list and is not a technical noun
or verb, choose the plainest, most concrete common word that covers the needed meaning,
commit to it for the rest of the document, and treat it as an ad hoc addition to this list
for that session.

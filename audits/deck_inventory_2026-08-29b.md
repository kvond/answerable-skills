### Advisory 1 — move-1 questions with no contrast device

A question that names the aspect and waits for the Contrast Set is
structurally "how are polar bears adapted?". Verbatim text below — these are
borderline calls and they are the teacher's to make, not this script's.

**03 — Energy Flow & Trophic Pyramids, S8**

> In any food chain, some living things can make their own food and some cannot. Which ones are the doorway that lets the sun's energy into the whole chain?

**03 — Energy Flow & Trophic Pyramids, S19**

> When one animal eats another, only a small part of that food's energy gets stored in the eater's body. What happens to all the rest of it?

**05 — Photosynthesis, S8**

> The Sun's energy arrives as light. The plant's food is a sugar. What has to happen to that light for it to end up stored inside the food?

**05 — Photosynthesis, S18**

> About 400 years ago Van Helmont planted a 5 lb willow tree in 200 lb of dried soil and gave it only water for 5 years. The tree ended up weighing 169 lb — it gained 164 lb. The soil weighed about 199.9 lb; it lost only about 2 ounces. If almost none of the tree came from the soil, where did those pounds of wood, leaves, and sugar actually come from?

**07a — Population Ecology, S8**

> Twenty-four rabbits became hundreds of millions in about 70 years — a curve shooting straight up. No population in the wild keeps that up forever. What makes a curve like that bend over and flatten?

**09 (review) — Cell Membrane & Transport, S18**

> The membrane is built from tiny molecules called phospholipids. Each one has a head that loves water and two tails that fear water. Because there is water inside AND outside the cell, the heads face out toward the water and the tails tuck together in the middle — two rows lined up, a bilayer. That leaves the middle of the membrane an oily, water-fearing zone. How could that one fact decide what is allowed to cross?

**13— Mendelian Genetics, S8**

> You are looking at a purple pea flower. Exactly which alleles is it carrying inside — and can your eyes alone tell you?

**16a — Stem Cells and Differentiation, S19**

> Not all stem cells can become every cell type. Can every stem cell become ANY tissue in the body?

**16c — Mutations and Genetic Disorders, S8**

> A sunburn damages DNA in skin cells. That damage stays with you for life. So why can a child never inherit it?

**19 — Speciation & Biodiversity, S8**

> One group of a single species gets cut in two by a new mountain range. Thousands of years later, the two sides can no longer breed together. What had to happen in between to turn one species into two?

**19 — Speciation & Biodiversity, S18**

> You meet a creature you have never seen before. There are millions of species already named, and to make sense of them we put them in groups. What would you most want to know about your creature to place it — and what does a good grouping give you that a random pile of facts does not?

# The Snake Question

*Front matter for the core questions document. This case comes first. The
specification comes after it.*

---

## How to read this

You will be given a moment from a Biology classroom. Everything the teacher had
is here. What she did is not, and it is deliberately not, until you have decided
what you would do.

## What is actually being asked

Losing legs contradicts the model most students carry, in which evolution
improves things. That model is not a gap in what Lezir knows. It is a working
theory, and his question is the sound of it failing against a case.

Resolving it takes two things held at once: that limbs impede movement in some
substrates, so losing them was a locomotor advantage; and that the trait spread
because those individuals left more offspring, not because it was better in any
general sense. Neither one alone settles it. A student who reasons only from
"limbs help you move" gets the wrong answer every time.

[A distinction worth keeping clean in front of students: differential
reproduction is the mechanism. Survival matters only where it feeds reproduction.
A survival-first framing reinforces the improvement model you are trying to
dislodge, which makes it the wrong shortcut in this cycle specifically.]

---

## What you have to decide, and it is four things at once

**The content.** The question is larger than the cycle. Answering it properly
takes both aspects above and probably ten minutes.

**His thinking.** He is holding a contradiction, not missing a fact, and he
found it himself. That is a different state from not knowing, and it does not
last.

**Him, in this room.** He does not speak. What happens to that if the question
gets handed to the class. What happens if it lands back on him for elaboration.
What happens if the answer is "good question" and the lesson continues.

**The design.** The cycle had a critical aspect under variation and somewhere it
was going. His question either extends that aspect, sits beside it, or cuts
across it. There are twenty-five minutes left.

---

## Your move

Choose one. Then write down which of the four you weighted, and what you gave up.

**A.** Answer it yourself, briefly and correctly, and return to the plan. Costs
the least time. Treats his question as a request for information.

**B.** Hand it to the class as an open question and run it for ten minutes. Costs
the design. Risks his one contribution becoming everyone's material.

**C.** Turn it back to him — "what do you think happened?" Keeps it his. Risks
putting a boy who does not speak on the spot in front of thirty people.

**D.** Hold it for the next class as the opening question. Protects the design.
Costs the moment, which will not still be live on Thursday.

**E.** Say you do not know, and find out with them, now.

[There is no right answer here. There is a defensible answer for this student,
this class, this cycle. That is what the rest of this document is built to
support.]

## What she did

[Katherine writes this. It includes the board. Say plainly that you did not know
snakes had once had legs, and that you looked it up in front of the class.]

[The reason that detail belongs here and not as an aside: it is a fifth thing
being coordinated, and it is the one teachers will feel hardest, because most
training says never show the gap. What made it work is that it enacted the
epistemic move the lesson was about. A question worth having is one you settle by
going to evidence. Your own not-knowing demonstrated that more convincingly than
any content delivery could have. A reader who is told to value student questions
and a reader who watches a teacher's authority survive not-knowing are receiving
different instruction.]

---

## Why the specification follows

Read cold, the core questions are a list of requirements, and the reasonable
teacher question is what am I being asked to comply with.

Read after this, they are the answer to a problem you have now felt. The design
half of the moment — the aspect, the contrast, the case that strains the rule —
is what the specification builds. It is built so there is room left for the other
three, which are yours and cannot be specified by anyone.

---

## Running this in PD

Present the moment. Ask: **what kinds of decisions is this teacher making right
now?**

Do not give the four layers first. Let the room produce them. Then show the four
as confirmation of what they just generated.

[This is also a live test of the frame. If a room of teachers names a layer
outside the four, the frame is incomplete and you have found it out cheaply.]

## Welcome

This is a full year of high school biology, and this page is the way in.

The year is built around one question: what makes a student answerable for their own thinking? Everything else follows from that. The course runs as twenty cycles rather than as chapters. In each cycle students commit to an answer before they are ready, and the work of the cycle is what changes that answer. Nothing here asks you to teach differently than you already know how to teach. It asks you to arrange the three days so that a student's first answer is worth revisiting.

You do not need to read the packet front to back, and you should not try to. Start wherever your question is. If you have never seen the system, read *What This Is* and then *Teaching One Cycle* — together they take about twenty minutes and they are enough to run your first cycle. If students arrive Monday, go straight to *Setting Up* and *The First Week*. Everything else is reference, and reference is for looking things up rather than reading through.

## A few words you will meet early

**Cycle.** One core idea run all the way through. Usually three class blocks rather than a week. The cycle, not the day, is the unit you plan in.

**CORE deck and EXTEND deck.** The CORE deck carries a cycle on its own and is a complete path. The EXTEND deck is a second deck for classes with room to go further. Four cycles offer you that choice — 7, 12, 15, and 16 — and you make it when you get there rather than now.

**DRAFT slide.** The one slide in each deck where a student's first and revised answers sit side by side. Its speaker notes hold the AI coaching prompt students run on Day 3.

**Fiddle.** A short hands-on task a student can pick up at the start of class without being told how. Most have a version that needs no technology.

**Variation theory.** The idea that a student learns what something is by experiencing what changes about it against what stays the same. This is why the decks are sequenced the way they are, and it is explained properly in the first section rather than assumed.

## How to use this packet

Read the two orientation pieces. Run one cycle. Then come back and read *Grading* and *AI Feedback*, which will make far more sense once you have a set of student decks in front of you than they do now.

The rest is reference. You are not behind for not having read it.

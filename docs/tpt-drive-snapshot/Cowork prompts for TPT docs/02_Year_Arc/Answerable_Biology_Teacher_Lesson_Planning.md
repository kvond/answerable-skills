# Answerable Biology — Teacher Lesson Planning Notes

*Working notes for how the year is grouped and paced. This is teacher-facing — the "cycle" language lives here, not on the product listings.*

## The cycle system

I group the biology year into **20 cycles**. Each cycle is built around **one major topic** — two if a class can hold them — and runs about **a week to a week and a half**.

The extra half-week is deliberate, not slippage. My classes are built for **resistant learners**, who need time to move through the loop that makes the thinking stick: commit to a first answer, work the idea through contrasts and a pattern-break, then revise. A faster class can finish a cycle in a week; mine take a week and a half. Grouping into cycles rather than fixed daily lessons is what gives that extra time a place to live — students get a major idea per week, and the pacing flexes to the class instead of the class racing the pacing guide.

## Pacing decisions inside a cycle

Some cycles carry **more than one lesson deck** (labeled a / b / c / d). These are not a required sequence — they are pace and depth options:

- The **core deck** carries the cycle's essential idea. Use it alone for a resistant-learner pace.
- The **additional decks** extend the cycle for classes that move faster or want more depth. Add as many as the week allows.

Teachers may have to choose which decks to run, or their classes may move faster than mine — either way, the cycle expands or contracts with the extra lessons rather than breaking.

# Set Up Answerable Biology

Purpose: the technical onboarding — what to open, decide, configure, and test, in the order you will actually do it, so you can begin Cycle 1 without rebuilding the curriculum first.

## How this setup is meant to work

The Master Agenda is the navigation for the whole year. Every cycle's entry names its deck file, links the canonical copy on the shared cloud drive, and lists the materials, fiddle, and supplies for that cycle. The Unit Agendas do the same for one unit at a time, at teaching depth. Everything else — sims, activity guides, card-set PDFs, videos — is linked from the deck slides themselves: the deck you project already carries its own links.

That means the one thing you should not do is download the whole drive and reorganize it into your own folder system. The curriculum is already organized; the agendas are the index. Duplicating it breaks the links, orphans you from corrections, and costs you days you do not have. You copy only what students must be able to type into (their deck copies) and whatever your school requires inside your LMS. Everything else stays where it is and gets opened from a link.

During class you have two things open: the deck, and the Master Agenda or the current Unit Agenda. That is the system.

A note if you have only the free unit (Cycle 02 — Ecosystems & Feeding Relationships, plus the Free Unit Guide, the Year Arc, and Start Here): the setup below works for the steps the free unit's four components support — do steps 1, 3, 5–7, and 11–12 for that single cycle and you have a complete, honest trial of the whole system. The Free Unit Guide stands in for the Unit Agenda in step 4 and carries the Cycle 02 supply list from step 10; step 2's Schedule Options, step 8's assessment document, and step 9's teacher batch workflow ship with the paid units — the completion rule you need is printed on the deck's closing checklist, and the student side of step 9 is already built into the response slides.

## The setup, in working order

Each step is tagged MUST DO BEFORE TEACHING or CAN BE BUILT AS YOU GO. Two of the must-dos (steps 8 and 9) technically have until your first Day 3, not Day 1 — but the first Day 3 arrives fast, so they are in the must-do list.

### Step 1 — Open Start Here

MUST DO BEFORE TEACHING. Read "Start Here" once, front to back. It is the ten-minute version of how the curriculum thinks: critical aspects, the first-answer → contrast → rule → pattern-break → revision arc, completion vs. proficiency, the human-gated AI loop. Every later step makes more sense with it read. Do not skip it to get to the files.

### Step 2 — Choose your schedule implementation

MUST DO BEFORE TEACHING. Open "Schedule Options" and find your bell schedule among the three worked examples: alternating A/B blocks (the native rhythm the decks were built on), daily 90-minute blocks, or 45-minute periods. What you are deciding:

- How many meetings a 3-block cycle takes on your calendar (three A/B meetings; three daily blocks; roughly 5–6 short periods).
- Where you will cut when your schedule forces a cut. The rule worth memorizing now: cut at a day-divider or after a response slide, and keep rule-building and its pattern-break in the same sitting.
- Nothing else. This is not a pacing commitment — the cycles are flexible by design, and the slack in the year is structural.

### Step 3 — Open the Year Arc

MUST DO BEFORE TEACHING. Skim the whole "Year Arc" once so you can see the four arcs (Ecology 01–07, Cells 08–11, Genetics 12–16, Evolution & Human Impact 17–20) and the year-at-a-glance table. Read only Cycles 01 and 02 closely — they are what you are teaching first. Do not decide CORE vs. EXTEND for the six deck-choice cycles (07, 08, 12, 13, 15, 16) now; you will know your class's pace by the time each one arrives, and "Use the core deck alone for a resistant-learner pace, or add the second to extend" is a decision best made then.

### Step 4 — Open the current Unit Agenda

MUST DO BEFORE TEACHING. Open the Unit Agenda for your first unit. It is the page you teach from: central question, critical aspects verbatim, the three days, activities, fiddle, supplies, and the Day-3 workflow, on one readable page. If an agenda for your unit does not exist yet, copy the blank Unit Agenda Template (in the Unit Guides folder) and fill it from the deck's teacher-reference slide and the matching Year Arc entry — about twenty minutes. For Cycles 01–07, the "Ecology Unit Guide" carries the full teaching depth behind the agenda; read the Cycle 01 and 02 sections before Day 1 if you have time, and the rest as those cycles approach.

### Step 5 — Decide: teach from linked resources, or copy selected materials into your LMS

MUST DO BEFORE TEACHING (the decision — the copying itself can trail). The default, and the recommendation, is to teach from the linked canonical files. Copy into your LMS only where something forces you to:

Stays linked, never copied: the agendas; the sims and tank models; the activity guides and card-set PDFs (print from the canonical file); the videos; the deck you project (present it straight from the drive).

Must be copied no matter what: the student deck. Each student types a first answer and a revised answer into response slides and runs the revision prompt from the slide's speaker Notes — so each student needs their own editable copy of the cycle's deck. That is the one non-negotiable duplication in the system, and it is per-student, not per-teacher.

Copy if your school requires it: some schools require all student-facing material to live inside the LMS. If yours does, copy the deck and the printed handouts for the current cycle only, cycle by cycle — never the whole drive at once. The Master Agenda remains your index either way.

### Step 6 — Set up student access

MUST DO BEFORE TEACHING (for Cycle 1; repeats per cycle after that — see step 12). Each student needs three things:

1. Their own editable copy of the current deck, with speaker Notes visible. The decks are .pptx. If your school runs Google, upload the deck to Drive and open with Google Slides — the speaker Notes (where the AI revision prompt lives) survive the conversion; spot-check one response slide's Notes after converting. If your school runs Office 365, distribute the .pptx directly.
2. A place to turn the finished deck in. Katherine's documented pattern is a class folder — the student-facing checklist in the decks reads "I turned the deck in to the folder." A Schoology assignment folder, a Google Classroom assignment, or a shared Drive folder all serve.
3. Device access to the linked sims (BioMan, the tank models, OneZoom, PhET). Nothing to install; they run in the browser. (Tank-model status: all nine github.io tank models are currently 404 — LINK NEEDED; only Cycle 02's Netlify energy model is live.) Whether your school's content filter allows them is what step 11 checks.

The distribution mechanism — how each student gets their individual copy (Schoology individual-copy assignment, Google Classroom "make a copy for each student," or manual duplication) — is not documented in the source materials beyond the submit-to-folder pattern. ### Step 7 — Configure assignments

MUST DO BEFORE TEACHING (Cycle 1 only; the rest as you go). Katherine's working pattern, visible on the decks' own teacher slides ("Graded activity (assign via Schoology): [ADD LINK]"), is two assignments per cycle:

1. The deck submission — one assignment per cycle, due end of Day 3. This is the completion artifact: first answer beside revised answer, both showing.
2. The graded activity — one assignment per cycle where the cycle has one (the food-web build in Cycle 02, the station checksheet in Cycle 03, the lab sheet in Cycle 05), linked from the deck's teacher slide and the Unit Agenda.

For Cycle 02 concretely: create the deck-submission assignment and, if you grade the food-web build, one assignment for it. Then paste both links into the "ADD LINK" lines of your Unit Agenda — the agenda template tells you the same thing: fill the link lines before Day 1; that is the one part no document can do for you.

One standing policy to configure while you are here: notes are due before students leave. Her stated reason, printed to students in the decks: "When you wait until later: you remember less, you're more likely to copy, you miss the learning that the notes are designed to create." Set due dates to the class period, not to midnight.

The exact Schoology click-path for creating individual-copy assignments and folders is not in the source materials — GAP — NOT IN SOURCE NOTES; WORKFLOW DEMONSTRATION NEEDED.

### Step 8 — Configure completion and proficiency gradebook categories where needed

MUST DO BEFORE your first Day 3. The system's default is one gradebook category: completion. "Completion = both answers present. Your growth shows in your printed Growth Report, not the grade." Proficiency is read from the Growth Reports and class summary, not entered as a column. Keep the grades heavy enough to matter — her words: "I am not going to pretend ninth-graders will engage purely for intrinsic reasons, and they need the grades to navigate the system."

If your school requires a graded proficiency entry, the assessment document ("Assessment: Completion, Copying, and Proficiency," in the Assessment folder) lays out both implementations — Option A, completion-only as designed, and Option B, completion plus proficiency for schools that require it. Set up the category weights before you enter the first Cycle 1 completion marks so you are not re-weighting mid-quarter.

Beyond the completion rule, gradebook structure (categories, weights, column naming) is not documented in Katherine's own materials — GAP — NOT IN SOURCE NOTES; the options in the assessment document are the build's derivation. Specific Schoology gradebook configuration: WORKFLOW DEMONSTRATION NEEDED.

### Step 9 — Configure the AI feedback workflow

MUST DO BEFORE your first Day 3. Two sides, and only one of them needs setup work:

Student side — already built. The revision prompt lives in the speaker Notes of every response slide; the student opens the Notes on Day 3, runs the prompt against their own first answer, and writes their revised answer under the first. The prompt is standardized: the AI quotes one thing right back, asks two questions, names one distinction — and never gives the answer. What you must settle before Day 3 is which AI tool your students will paste the prompt into, under your school's AI policy. The sources do not name a specific student-facing tool, deliberately — her caveat is "I am not describing a product... The principle is portable; the particular way I do it probably is not." REVIEW WITH KATHERINE for the recommended student tool; WORKFLOW DEMONSTRATION NEEDED for the student-side run-through on a school device.

Teacher side — the batch step. After decks come in, you run the finished decks through one batch step to produce a one-page Conceptual Growth Report per student and a class summary for you. The full workflow, the prompts, and the feedback register live in the AI Feedback folder ("AI Feedback and Conceptual Growth") — read it before your first Day 3, not after. The operational batch procedure itself (collecting decks, running the batch, printing reports): WORKFLOW DEMONSTRATION NEEDED.

The non-negotiable, whichever tools you use: you read every report before it goes to a student. "That is not a small caveat. It is the whole architecture."

### Step 10 — Check supplies

MUST DO BEFORE TEACHING (first cycles); then as you go. Open the "Master Supply System" (Supplies folder) — it carries the per-cycle checklists and the year shopping view. Before Day 1 you need only the first cycles' print-and-gather list; for the ecology arc that is nearly all paper and devices:

- Cycle 01: labeled cups for the 7 Linnaean levels (one set per lab station), printed pond-dip lab sheets, HHMI answer documents.
- Cycle 02: printed Pacific NW species card sets (one per group), food-web sheets, markers for arrows.
- Cycle 03: printed station cards and checksheets.

The year's first real lab is Cycle 05 (Leaf-Disk Float — baking soda, dish soap, fresh spinach, needleless syringes, clear cups, a lamp, goggles), so your first actual shopping lead time is weeks out, not days. Put a recurring fifteen minutes at the start of each cycle to check the next cycle's list; the fresh items (spinach, the borrowed fish in Cycle 06, liver in Cycle 10) are the only ones that cannot be bought ahead.

### Step 11 — Test student view

MUST DO BEFORE TEACHING. Log in as a student — or borrow a student device and a test account — and run the first cycle's path end to end:

1. Open the distributed deck copy. Can you type into a response slide? Can you open the speaker Notes and see the revision prompt? (In Google Slides: View > Show speaker notes, or drag the notes bar up. In PowerPoint: View > Notes.)
2. Click every link on the slides you will use in Cycle 1–2: the fiddle (OneZoom for Cycle 01, BioMan for Cycle 02), the tank model, the Drive-hosted PDFs. You are checking two failure modes — school content filters blocking a sim, and Drive permission walls on shared files. The activity-guides folder linked from the Cycle 09 deck is already flagged "confirm public sharing" (REVIEW WITH KATHERINE), and two sims are flagged "verify it runs" (the legacy PhET Membrane Channels and the peppered-moths game); assume any link can fail until a student account has opened it.
3. Submit the deck to the class folder or assignment and confirm it arrives.

Ten minutes now saves you a first-day block spent doing live tech support in front of thirty students.

### Step 12 — Begin Cycle 1

You are set up. On the day: open the Master Agenda to the Cycle 01 entry, open the fiddle tab before the bell (OneZoom — "find a pair that look almost the same but are far apart on the tree"), project the deck, and teach. Note that Cycle 01 (Classifying Organisms) is a legacy-format deck — its teacher slide is slide 2, "Teacher Navigation," not slide 1; the Master Agenda entry walks through its quirks.

From here on, setup collapses into a short per-cycle loop, fifteen minutes before each cycle starts:

1. Open the next Unit Agenda (or fill one from the template).
2. Create the cycle's deck-submission assignment and graded-activity assignment; paste the links into the agenda.
3. Distribute student deck copies.
4. Print and gather from the Master Supply System checklist.
5. Queue the fiddle tab.

That loop is the whole ongoing cost of the system's administration. Everything else — Growth Report batches on Day 3, the occasional CORE/EXTEND decision, supply shopping ahead of labs — happens on the rhythm the cycles set.

# Fiddles, Extensions, and YouTube

Purpose: what Fiddles are and how to run them, plus three catalogs you can pull from all year — every Fiddle by cycle (with no-tech alternatives), every challenge and extension by cycle, and a curated YouTube list with the reason each video earns its link.

This is the reference catalog. The day-to-day slot for each Fiddle is already printed on the Master Agenda and each Unit Agenda; the ecology cycles (01–07) are treated in full teaching depth in the Ecology Unit Guide; printable no-tech materials (cards, cutouts, coloring sheets) are inventoried in the Master Supply System. If you are new to the system, read Start Here first.

## 1. What a Fiddle is

A Fiddle is a small exploration — usually a sim, sometimes cards or a puzzle — that is already open when students walk in. It is on the board or on their Chromebooks before the bell, it needs no directions beyond one line, and it points at the same idea the lesson is about to teach. Katherine's own deck slides name the slot exactly: "Fiddle sim (open for students on arrival)" (Genes & Chromosomes teacher navigation slide) and "Explore first (fiddle)" (both Cycle 13 decks). Her timing notes on the Darwin deck give the rhythm: sim entry 5–7 minutes, bellringer 3–5, then teaching.

One Fiddle covers a surprising number of real-classroom jobs:

- Bell-ringer exploration — students meet the cycle's idea by poking at it before anyone explains it. The Cycle 17 fiddle asks exactly that: "What changes when you add mutations or change the environment? What stays the same?"
- Attendance time — the first minutes of a block are yours for roll, late passes, and the student who needs a word. The room is already working.
- Transitions — between lab cleanup and the next slide, between Day 2's activity and the concept question. The Cycle 18 Peppered Moths game runs in one-minute rounds; it is transition-sized by design.
- Early-finisher work — the student who is done has somewhere real to go that is still this cycle's content, not a puzzle sheet.
- Extension — most Fiddles have a deeper layer (the PhET fiddle becomes the Bunny Lab; OneZoom becomes the convergent-evolution hunt).
- An alternative to phones and unrelated Chromebook use — the open tab competes for the same idle minutes, and it is the lesson.
- Breathing room for slower students — when faster students have a Fiddle and a challenge to reach for, you can give a slower student ten more minutes without dragging the class. This is the pace principle in her words: "the pace itself often destroys the possibility of depth."

Why this is not busywork: the Fiddle's variable is usually the cycle's critical aspect itself. Her sim reasoning: "What makes the sim work is not that it is interactive or digital. It is that the variable they manipulate is the critical aspect itself. ... They are not being asked to memorize a dependency; they are given an object that lets them feel it." Messing around is a legitimate learning phase in this system, not off-task time — the fiddle is where it happens on purpose.

One note, once: the Fiddle structure is Katherine's own — the labeled arrival-sim slot appears in her decks, and "fiddle tabs" appear in her book notes among the fine-grained agency supports. The fuller rationale laid out above (phones, attendance coverage, breathing room) is how the build uses the structure; it is not yet written out in her own words. REVIEW WITH KATHERINE.

Running it: open the tab (or set out the cards) before students arrive; one line of instruction on the slide; do not debrief it — it feeds the bellringer, which feeds the first-answer question. Every Fiddle below has a no-tech alternative for the day the wifi dies, the cart is missing, or a student has lost device privileges.

## 3. Catalog 2 — Challenges and extensions by cycle

Extension in this system is built into the decks, not bolted on. Three layers, no new logistics:

- The Mastery tier of every three-tier concept question — the same question, deeper demand; students self-select.
- The "Keep going" stretch — explicitly no-lookup ("There's no answer to look up. Think it through and take your best guess."), and deliberately threaded: most Keep-goings are the next cycle's question in disguise. Protect the thread when you compress or reorder — the chain is mapped in the Year Arc.
- The Day-3 optional challenge / What-if — the EXPLORE/CHALLENGE layer. These live inside the decks as optional slides, not as separate products.

Her reasoning for why this layer exists: "Much of teaching is making the harder thing available without forcing it, and reading which student is ready to reach for it on which day." Challenges are made available, not assigned; they are not graded for correctness — completion and the Conceptual Growth Report carry the record (see the Assessment documentation).

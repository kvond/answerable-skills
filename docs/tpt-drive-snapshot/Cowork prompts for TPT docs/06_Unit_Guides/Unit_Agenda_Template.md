# Unit Agenda Template

Purpose: the reusable one-per-unit operating document — copy the blank template below, fill it from the deck's teacher-reference slide and the Year Arc, and teach from it. It should be readable during class.

A Unit Agenda is not a script and not a pacing guide. It holds the unit's questions, the links you will open, the materials you must have ready, and the Day-3 revision workflow — nothing else. Theory and rationale live in Start Here; the year view lives in the Year Arc and Master Agenda; calendar placement lives in Schedule Options; the ecology cycles get full teaching depth in the Ecology Unit Guide.

## How to fill this in

Open the unit's deck(s) and copy from the teacher-reference slide (slide 1) and the Teacher Navigation slide if present: cycle number, essential claim, critical aspects, and any Schoology/activity links already stamped there. Pull the central question, activity list, fiddles, supplies, and links from the matching cycle entry in the Year Arc — the links are inventoried there, with dead ones flagged (— 404, LINK NEEDED and the like) and flags where something is missing. Keep every field to the length shown in the example; if a field wants to grow past a few lines, the extra belongs in the Ecology Unit Guide (or a future unit guide), not here. Leave the flags in (LINK NEEDED, SOURCE NOT YET LOCATED, and the rest) — an agenda that admits what is missing beats one that pretends. Fill the Schoology link lines before Day 1; that is the one part no document can do for you.

# Unit Agenda — Ecosystems & Feeding Relationships

Cycle(s): Cycle 02 — Ecosystems & Feeding Relationships (CORE; the only deck for this cycle)
Blocks: 3 (deck's own dates: Sep 07–18 on an A/B block calendar)

## Unit arc

Students start from their own dinner and end able to trace energy and matter through a food web they built themselves. Arc 1 establishes that energy thins out drastically at each feeding step; Arc 2 establishes that two species' relationship is defined by outcomes — who gains, who loses — not by appearances. The closing Keep-going ("why hasn't life run out of energy?") hands the problem to Cycles 03–05.

## Central question(s)

- What connects every organism in an ecosystem? Essential claim: "Organisms in an ecosystem are connected by the transfer of matter and energy through feeding relationships."
- Critical aspects: "Critical aspect: Energy flow through trophic levels" · "Critical aspect: Symbiosis and species relationships"

## Lessons (decks)

- Cycle 02 — Ecosystems & Feeding Relationships (VT deck, v2), 32 slides — Schoology link: ADD LINK
- Day 1 — the most teaching: energy flow. First answer ("At each step, do you get all of the grass's energy — or something else?"), the 10,000-calorie meadow vs. 10–15 hawks contrast, the rule, the decomposer pattern-break. Close by starting the food-web build.
- Day 2 — shorter lesson, longer activity: symbiosis. Bee-and-flower vs. mosquito-and-human, the who-benefits rule, the bee-mimic orchid pattern-break. Finish the food web and its energy-pyramid math; bobtail squid investigation (~20 min).
- Day 3 — see your thinking change: revision on both response slides, then the What-if (all decomposers vanish — watch the decomposers video first) and the Break the Web challenge.

## Activities

- Two-day food-web build — students build a web from Pacific NW species cards, then do the 10%-rule energy-pyramid math on it — cards PDF: https://drive.google.com/file/d/17heyT0Y6KVcezjak08wq7Zr8SSNaJyxg/view
- Living Together: the bobtail squid — ~20 min observational investigation; What does the squid get? What do the bacteria get? — https://www.biointeractive.org/classroom-resources/living-together
- Chains & Webs (Gorongosa) — cited in deck; LINK NEEDED (only the biointeractive.org root is in the deck)

## Labs

(No wet lab this cycle. The year's first real lab, with the safety introduction, is Cycle 05.)

## Fiddles

- BioMan Ecology Games on arrival — remove one species from a food web, see what happens — https://biomanbiology.com — dead domain, use biomanbio.com, LINK NEEDED
- Energy Tank Model slider play — https://hilarious-biscuit-630a42.netlify.app (pre-migration URL; later cycles use answerable-teaching.github.io — REVIEW WITH KATHERINE)
- Early finishers: Break the Web ("is there any single species whose removal would barely change the web?")

## Feedback and revision (Day 3)

- Students reopen slides 15 and 25, run the AI revision prompt in each slide's speaker Notes, and write the revised answer under the first — both stay showing.
- Students submit the finished deck to the class folder / Schoology: ADD LINK.
- You batch the decks → one Conceptual Growth Report per student + your class summary. Read every report before it goes out.

## Assessments

- Completion: both answers present on each response slide (2 response slides: energy flow; symbiosis type). Enter as completion only — "Completion = both answers present. Your growth shows in your printed Growth Report, not the grade."
- Proficiency: read from the Growth Reports and class summary — who can trace energy through a web, and who is still grouping organisms by what they are rather than by how they feed.
- Three-tier concept questions in-deck: slide 14 (energy) and slide 24 (symbiosis), Getting Started / Working On It / Mastery, word bank on the slide.

## Supplies

- Print: Pacific NW species card sets (one per group), food-web sheets
- Gather: markers or pencils for arrows
- Devices needed for: BioMan, Energy Tank Model, bobtail squid investigation, decomposers video

## Optional follow-ups

- Break the Web challenge (slide 30) — remove one species, trace the arrows, predict one year and one generation out
- Decomposers video before the What-if — https://youtu.be/7cRgK0qG00E
- The Keep-going ("If energy is lost at every step and never comes back, why hasn't life run out of energy? There's no answer to look up.") bridges to Cycle 03 — Energy Flow & Trophic Pyramids

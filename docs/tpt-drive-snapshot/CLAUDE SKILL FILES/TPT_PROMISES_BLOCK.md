## What I changed, and what I did not

Your eight headers are yours, unaltered. Four mechanical edits:

- **"EDITABLE GOOGLE SLIDES"** — "Teachers can change pacing" became "Change pacing."
  Every other line addresses the reader directly; this one described them in the third
  person.
- **"SEE WHAT YOU'RE BUYING"** — your line reads *"Show actual lesson slides…"*, which is
  an instruction to yourself, not a promise to the buyer. Turned to face the reader.
- Line breaks so nothing wraps badly in TPT's description box, which is narrow.
- Rules above and below, because TPT descriptions run as one undifferentiated column.

Two blocks added, both bracketed proposals — cut either.

**[WHAT THE DOWNLOAD ACTUALLY IS]** — placed first, deliberately. Complaint 5 is that
buyers cannot tell what they are getting, and complaint 4 is non-editable PDFs. Your
product is a Digital Download whose file is a PDF, so a buyer skimming the listing sees
exactly the two things they have been burned by. Left unaddressed, that is the single
likeliest source of a one-star review on this store, and it would be a review about a
misunderstanding rather than about the work. Saying it in the first four lines converts
the objection before it forms.

**[HUMAN-GATED, CLASSROOM-TESTED]** — complaint 2, AI slop, is the newest and the
sharpest. The instinct is to answer it by claiming no AI was involved, and that would be
false here: AI helped find the activities. Claiming otherwise is the overclaim buyers hunt
for, and one teacher noticing it would cost more than the claim ever earned.

The honest version is stronger anyway. What buyers actually object to in complaint 2 is
not that a machine was in the room — it is *unreviewed* output: passages that make no
sense, questions that cannot be answered, activities citing simulation features that do
not exist. Every one of those is a failure of **review**, not of authorship. So the claim
that answers the complaint is not "no AI touched this," it is "nothing here reached you
unreviewed." Human-gated and classroom-tested say exactly that, and they are true.

## One claim to check before you publish

**"CLASSROOM-TESTED."** Buyers punish that phrase harder than any other when it turns out
to mean "designed for a classroom" rather than "run with students." Have all twenty cycles
been taught to actual students, or is 2026–27 the first full run?

If some have and some have not, the honest and stronger version names the number:
*"Every cycle in this unit has been taught to my own students"* if true — or, if not,
*"Built and refined in my own classroom over N years"*, which claims the experience
without claiming a run that has not happened. [You have 35 years of teaching and a
doctorate. You do not need the borderline claim; the specific one is more persuasive than
the generic one anyway.]

# Simultaneity — Research Base and Positioning

*For the teaching chapter, then the fusion chapter. Ordering note at the end.*

---

## 1. The construct

Marton's term is **simultaneity**: holding more than one thing in awareness at
once. It divides two ways, and your two artifacts map onto the division exactly.

**Synchronic simultaneity.** Two or more critical aspects present in the same
case, in the same moment, so the learner must coordinate them to resolve it.
This is fusion in the strict sense. The compensatory pair and the conflict case
do this.

**Diachronic simultaneity.** Things encountered at different times brought into
awareness together, so relations between them become discernible for the first
time. The Concept Bank does this. Nothing new is introduced; a sequence becomes a
structure.

Both are coordination work. They differ in what has to be held together and in
what makes it hard. Diachronic is the harder of the two to engineer and most
curricula do not attempt it.

---

## 2. The diagnostic argument, and it is the strongest practical claim here

Teachers say a student does not **conceptualize** it. That is a verdict with no
next move attached, so the response defaults to more review or more practice,
neither of which addresses either possible cause.

The distinction breaks the verdict into two failures with different remedies:

- **Did she discern the aspects?** (separation) → the remedy is another contrast.
- **Did she coordinate them when the case required both?** (fusion) → the remedy
  is a case that will not resolve on one aspect.

Same observed performance, opposite instruction.

[This is the case for putting the vocabulary in the teacher package. You are not
handing adopting teachers theory for its own sake. You are handing them the terms
that turn an undifferentiated judgment into an addressable one.]

[On naming: separation and fusion are Marton's and carry his framework, which
costs something if a teacher has to buy the framework to use the distinction. The
plain-language alternatives are worse — "did she see the parts" and "did she put
them together" sound like the same thing twice, and that vagueness is exactly what
is being removed. Keep the technical terms in teacher-facing material with a
one-line gloss. Keep them out of anything a student reads entirely.]

---

## 3. The claim about review sheets

Curricula attempt diachronic simultaneity through review sheets and cumulative
tests. Co-presence is achieved, in the trivial sense that the terms are on one
page. What is not achieved is discernible relation, because filling a blank
exercises retrieval, and retrieval leaves the sequence a sequence.

A student who fills in a review sheet and then answers a synchronic question is
exercising her thinking. A student who fills in a review sheet and is then tested
on it is exercising her memory of concepts under testing pressure.

[This is a testable claim and it is the kind a reviewer will want evidence for.
The retrieval practice literature is large and largely favorable to review and
testing, so the argument cannot be that retrieval does nothing. It has to be that
retrieval consolidates what was separated and does not by itself produce
coordination. Framed that way it is compatible with the evidence and still says
something.]

---

## 4. What this changes in feedback

Feedback runs through prompts shipped with the curriculum. That is the reason the
diagnostic can travel: an adopting teacher can run a prompt, and could never run
someone else's pipeline.

If a first submission and a rewrite are both scored on conceptual accuracy, the
same thing is measured twice and the rewrite becomes a correction exercise.

Two questions instead of one, written into the prompt:

- Did the student discern the aspects?
- Did the student coordinate them when the case required both?

A student can be strong on the first and absent on the second. Under a single
accuracy score that reads as partial understanding rather than as a specific and
addressable gap. It is probably close to what you were already noticing:
a student who states both aspects accurately and never relates them is not failing
to know, and telling her to study is the wrong response to a wrong diagnosis.

**In what goes back to the student:** name what she discerned, then whether and how she
brought two things together, in ordinary language. **In the revision prompt:** a
separation gap gets another contrast; a coordination gap gets the case back with a
question that will not resolve on one aspect.

[The drafting problem: coordination language is harder to keep positive than
accuracy language. "You explained both clearly and I want to see what happens when
you put them together" works. Finding phrasings that do not read as "you did not
really understand" will take iteration, and that message is exactly what the
report exists to avoid.]

## 5. Where this sits in the field

**Variation Theory.** Marton and Booth, *Learning and Awareness*; Marton, *Necessary
Conditions of Learning*. The intended, enacted, and lived objects of learning are
the distinction that justifies the teacher note slide: your decks carry the
intended object, and a teacher substituting an example in good faith changes the
enacted one without knowing it.

**Learning study** literature (Lo, Pang, Ko) is the closest existing use of
Variation Theory as a teacher development method, and worth positioning against —
it develops judgment through collaborative cycles rather than through distributed
materials.

**Christian Moore-Anderson**, *Teaching Meaning: What Works When Telling Isn't
Enough* and *Difference Maker*. Variation Theory applied to biology specifically,
building on Marton and Tsui. Two contributions carried into your decks: the
question must contain the variation, and stock-and-flow as a question form for
rate against amount. He is a practitioner-author rather than peer-reviewed
literature — cite him for design lineage, and ground the underlying claims in
Marton and Tsui so a reviewer has something checkable.

**Dialogic.** Bakhtin on authoritative and internally persuasive discourse.
Nystrand, *Opening Dialogue*, for authentic questions and uptake at classroom
level. Matusov and *Dialogic Pedagogy* for the Bakhtinian line in education.

**Teacher judgment.** Lampert (2001) is the nearest model for holding content,
individual student, class, and design simultaneously. Hawkins (1967) for the
teacher–child–subject triangle, without the collective.

**Biology-specific.** Nehm on evolutionary reasoning; Jungck's problem-based work;
BioInteractive; Vision and Change. [Check these before writing biology's lack of a
reform artifact as a gap. A reviewer in biology education will know this
literature, and the argument is stronger engaging what exists than describing an
absence. My picture of the current state is not reliable enough to ground the
claim.]

## 6. What is yours

Variation Theory supplies separation and fusion but is largely silent on the
teacher's real-time coordination problem. Dialogic pedagogy supplies the account
of why a student speaks but declines to specify the move. Lampert renders the
complexity and leaves the coordination to the reader.

Your addition is **parameters**: the layers to weigh, the terms that make a vague
judgment addressable, and the tradeoff made visible — without prescribing the
move.

The second addition, and it may be the more original one: **the same construct
runs at both levels.** The student coordinates two aspects in a case. The teacher
coordinates content, student, class, and design in a moment. Simultaneity is the
demand in both, which is why materials that develop one can be built to develop
the other.

[The test on the parameters is not coherence. It is whether they fire under time
pressure with thirty students. A parameter that does not operate in a real case
does not belong in the book, which is an argument for developing them against the
case file rather than specifying them up front and testing after.]

## 7. Chapter order

Teaching chapter first, fusion chapter second, as you said. The reason it works:
the teaching chapter establishes simultaneity as the teacher's problem through
Lezir. The fusion chapter then shows the same construct as the student's problem
and as a design requirement. Reversed, fusion reads as a technique and the teacher
material reads as application.

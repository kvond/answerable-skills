# Visibility — The Missing Dimension

Building participation in a room where it does not yet exist. With readiness
gates, so an adopting teacher is not guessing.

---

## 1. What is actually missing, stated so it survives review

Adjacent work exists and should be cited rather than ignored.

- **Elizabeth Cohen**, *Designing Groupwork*, and the complex instruction line —
  status and participation inequality, treated directly.
- **Kimberly Tanner**, "Structure Matters" (LSE, 2013) — participation structures
  for classroom equity: wait time, random call versus hands, think-pair-share.
- **Accountable talk** (Michaels, O'Connor, Resnick) — norms for talk, including
  revoicing.
- **Lampert** — uses student work publicly, and thinks hard about what that costs
  the student.
- **Edmondson**, psychological safety — organizational rather than classroom.

**What none of them do:** treat exposure as a **graduated dimension with its own
clock**, sequenced across an arc, declared per cycle, and held separate from
cognitive demand. The techniques are offered as interchangeable and available
immediately.

That is why PD fails at this. A teacher is handed cold call or hands-up voting as
an engagement move, tries it in October with a class that has never seen a wrong
answer treated well, gets silence, and concludes her students are the problem.

[The originality has to be stated as a difference, not an absence. Rung 3 below
resembles revoicing and resembles what Lampert does with student work. The
difference is that they treat it as a discourse move a teacher makes when useful,
and this treats it as a scaffold with a position in a sequence and a precondition
for what follows. Say that, or a reviewer who knows revoicing will find you.]

## 2. The two scales, again, because this is the design error

**Visibility**, low to high: written and private → shown to one partner → read
aloud unattributed → owned aloud → public simultaneous commitment.

**Complexity**, low to high: one aspect → a rule → a strained rule → two aspects
coordinated → a counterfactual.

Different clocks. A hands-up vote is **low complexity at high visibility** — a
forced binary that exposes you to thirty people. That mismatch is why it fails
early and why it is not a starting move.

A high-complexity question is usually *easier* in a low-visibility form, because
the student needs thinking time more than an audience.

**Raise visibility across the year. Reset it low inside each cycle.** If
visibility rises monotonically across the arc, the highest-exposure moves land on
the hardest content, which is the worst possible pairing.

---

## 3. The ladder, with readiness gates

The rungs are gated by **observable evidence**, not by week number. This is what
lets an adopting teacher place the moves without a schedule, and without knowing
her class in advance.

The mechanism throughout: the student is gathering evidence about what happens to
wrong answers. She cannot get that evidence from being told.

---

### Rung 1 — Written, seen only by the teacher

*Precondition:* none, beyond not having punished wrongness.

*The structure:* two-tier response. First answer scored for completion. The
revision is where thinking counts.

**Ready for rung 2 when:** most students write a first answer at all, and some
first answers are wrong in interesting ways rather than blank or copied. A blank
is a refusal to commit even privately, which means rung 1 is not yet secure.

---

### Rung 2 — Written, shown to one assigned partner

*Assigned, not chosen.* Chosen partners reproduce the existing social order, and
the students most at risk choose nobody.

**Ready for rung 3 when:** pairs talk about the answer rather than about
something else, and at least some students change or extend an answer after
talking.

---

### Rung 3 — The teacher reads an answer aloud, unattributed

**This is the rung that does the work, and the one no PD teaches.**

Take a **wrong but productive** answer from the stack. Read it without a name.
Then *use* it — build on it, ask what it assumes, put it against a case. Do not
correct it and move on.

The class watches wrongness be valuable before anyone is identified with it.
Four or five instances change what a wrong answer means in that room.

*Requirements:* the answer is genuinely useful, not a straw man, because students
detect the difference immediately. Never signal whose it is, including by
looking at them.

**Ready for rung 4 when:** students volunteer answers you have not solicited, or
a student says a version of "that was mine" without distress.

---

### Rung 4 — A student owns an answer aloud, by invitation

*By invitation, privately arranged where possible:* "your answer about X is the
one I want to start with — will you say it?"

Available because she has watched rung 3 and knows what will be done with what
she says.

**Ready for rung 5 when:** more than a few students speak, disagreement happens
between students rather than through you, and a wrong answer aloud does not end
that student's participation for the period.

---

### Rung 5 — Public simultaneous commitment

The vote, pair talk, sequential hands, justification, re-vote. Moore-Anderson's
protocol.

Last, not first. It asks for commitment and exposure at once, and it only works
where the exposure has been made cheap by everything above.

---

### Descent

Any of these resets. A new class, a new semester, a hard week, a student who was
humiliated somewhere else that day. Drop a rung without announcing it.

[The gates are stated as observations rather than as measures on purpose. A
teacher can see all of them from the front of the room in one period, which is
the only kind of criterion that gets used. The cost is that they are not
research instruments and should not be presented as such.]

## 3b. Visibility mapped onto the nine moves

The question sequence already ramps exposure, and making that deliberate solves
the reset problem: each cycle restarts at move 1, so visibility resets low inside
the cycle while the ceiling rises across the year.

Each move carries a **maximum visibility** appropriate to what it asks. A teacher
operates at or below it, depending on where the room is.

| Move | Max visibility | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Critical Aspect question | Read aloud unattributed | A choice between named alternatives; both defensible |
| 2. Contrast Set | Read aloud unattributed, or vote | Cheapest place to be wrong, and the most productive |
| 3. Build a Rule | Partner | She authored it; it is hers in a way a choice is not |
| 4. Pattern Break | Written and private | Her own rule fails here. Do not do that publicly |
| 5. 3-Tier Question | Written and private | Tier choice reveals self-assessment |
| 6. Continuity question | Partner, or aloud | No right answer exists, so wrongness is not at stake |
| 7. Stock-and-flow | Partner, or group | Building a model is collaborative work |
| 8. Compensatory pair | Group | High complexity, low exposure — see 3c |
| 9. Conflict case | Group | Same |
| What-if | Written and individual | It is the evidence. See 3c |

**On cold call:** if a teacher uses it, this table tells her where it is safe.
Cold call at move 2 asks a student to pick between two options with a reason for
either. Cold call at move 4 asks her to watch her own rule break in front of
thirty people. The move, not the technique, is what makes it appropriate.

[Rejected, and worth recording so it does not come back: a per-student readiness
list. It is real information and it is also a written record that this student
cannot speak in front of the class. It follows her, it becomes a lens, and
Cohen's status finding is precisely that expectations become self-confirming. A
list is an expectation written down. Track by **move** instead — "the move 2
read-aloud is working; nobody yet owns an answer at move 4" — which tells the
teacher what to do next without classifying anyone. Individual knowledge stays
where it already is, in the teacher's head and in a private invitation.]

## 3c. Group work

Group work is a **visibility reducer**, not a rung. A student speaks to three
people instead of thirty, and the product carries the group's name. That
distributed accountability is most of why a class likes it, and it is not a bad
reason.

**The lever:** group work can carry higher complexity than the room's whole-class
rung allows. The compensatory pair and the conflict case can run in groups early.
You do not need rung 4 to ask a hard question — you need rung 4 to ask it in
front of everyone.

**The trap:** a group product says nothing about who coordinated. It can be
complete and correct with one student having done the relating and three having
watched. So group work is a good **occasion** for coordination and useless as
**evidence** of it.

**The rule that follows:** moves 8 and 9 may be group work. The what-if must be
written individually, or the diagnosis is empty.

Two cautions for the teacher note:

- Cohen's status finding operates *inside* groups, not only across the class. The
  student who does not speak in class often does not speak in the group either,
  and the product conceals it. Rotating roles help only if the role has real
  content.
- A class can enjoy group work indefinitely without moving up, because the group
  is a place to be quiet in. If group work is the main mode, rung 3 still has to
  happen separately on individual written answers, or rung 4 never opens.

[A class that has taken to group work is telling you rung 2 is secure — they are
getting something from a partner's thinking, which is the precondition for rung
3.]

## 4. Assessment, and why it sits under visibility

**The position:** do not test students on a concept until they can conceptualize
it — meaning until they can coordinate the aspects, not recite them. A test
administered before that measures separation and testing pressure, and the score
gets read as understanding.

A test is also **rung 5 without any of rung 3**. Maximum exposure, no evidence
gathered about what happens to a wrong answer, and the wrongness is permanent and
recorded. Everything the ladder is built to avoid, arriving in a single event.

**If an artifact is required** — and it usually is:

Build it from the accumulated feedback the curriculum prompts have already
produced across a cycle, rather than administering a new event. That record
already holds what the student discerned, whether she coordinated, and what
changed between first answer and revision. An AI pass over a term of it can
produce a leveled statement per student, on a larger evidence base than one
sitting.

*Requirements if this is built:*

- The compilation is a **read** over feedback the teacher has already reviewed.
  No new judgment is introduced by the model.
- Levels are stated as what she can do, not as a score with a cut point.
- The teacher reviews every compiled statement before it leaves.

[This depends on the curriculum prompts producing a per-student record that
persists across a cycle. If they return feedback to the student and retain
nothing, there is no accumulated evidence to compile and this section needs
rebuilding around whatever the prompts do keep.]

[The challenge, and it is the one an administrator will raise: this produces no
common instrument, so it cannot be compared across sections or teachers. That is
a genuine cost, not a rhetorical objection. The defensible answer is that the
compiled statement is more valid for the individual student and less useful for
aggregate comparison, and that the tradeoff is deliberate. Do not claim it does
both.]

## 5. The observation problem

A district observer arrives looking for active dialogue with vocabulary use.

A room at rung 1 or 2 shows quiet students writing. That reads as low engagement
against every walkthrough rubric in use, and it is the correct instruction for
that room at that time.

Three things worth having ready:

**The convergence is real, not a trick.** Rung 3 produces exactly what the rubric
wants — student ideas discussed aloud, terms used in reasoning — and produces it
in a room where a vote would produce silence. Where you are on the ladder is
where the observable dialogue is available.

**Name the rung in the teacher note.** A cycle that declares "visibility: written
and private, building toward unattributed read-aloud" converts a deficiency into
a stated design decision. An observer can disagree with a decision. She cannot
disagree with an absence, because an absence looks like a failure.

**The vocabulary point has a real answer.** Terms used in reasoning at rung 3
beat terms recited at rung 5. Vocabulary in a wrong answer being taken seriously
is vocabulary in use.

[Where this goes in the book: the observation problem is the clearest instance of
the argument. A rubric that scores visible participation without regard to what
it costs a student rewards teachers who front-load exposure and penalizes those
building the conditions for it. That is a motivation-model instrument applied to
agency-model work, at the institutional level, and it is the same error the
curriculum package risks making with teachers.]

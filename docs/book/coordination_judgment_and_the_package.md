# Answerable Teaching — Coordination, Judgment, and the Curriculum Package

*A sequenced account of work developed 2026-08-29. Written for communication, not
in the order it was thought.*

---

## 1. The problem this addresses

Teachers say a student does not **conceptualize** it. That is a verdict with no
next move attached. The default responses — more review, more practice — address
neither of the two things that could actually be wrong.

The same vagueness appears in curriculum design. A cycle can be well built,
active, and well received, and still leave students unable to use what they
learned, which then gets read as low effort or weak background.

Both problems have the same shape: an undifferentiated judgment where a
distinction is available.

---

## 2. The construct: simultaneity

Marton's term for holding more than one thing in awareness at once. It divides
two ways.

**Synchronic simultaneity.** Two or more critical aspects present in the same
case, in the same moment, so the learner must coordinate them to resolve it. This
is fusion in the strict sense.

**Diachronic simultaneity.** Things met at different times brought into awareness
together, so relations between them become discernible for the first time.
Nothing new is introduced; a sequence becomes a structure. The Concept Bank does
this. It is the harder of the two to engineer and most curricula do not attempt
it.

Both are coordination work. They differ in what has to be held together.

**The diagnostic that follows**, and it is the strongest practical result here:

| Question | Failure | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Did she discern the aspects? | Separation | Another contrast |
| Did she coordinate them when the case required both? | Fusion | A case that will not resolve on one aspect |

Same observed performance, opposite instruction. This is why the vocabulary
belongs in teacher-facing material: it converts a verdict into something
addressable.

## 3. The nine moves, and their lineage

Not nine questions. Nine thinking moves, each with a purpose and a source.
Lineage runs **Marton and Tsui → Moore-Anderson → von Duyke**.

**Five core questions**, once per critical aspect, in fixed order:

1. Critical Aspect question
2. Contrast Set
3. Build a Rule
4. Pattern Break
5. 3-Tier Question

**Four conditional structures**, used only where the content calls for them:

6. Continuity question
7. Stock-and-flow model
8. Compensatory pair
9. Conflict case

Marton supplies the patterns of variation — contrast, generalization, separation,
fusion — and the intended, enacted, and lived objects of learning.
Moore-Anderson supplies stock-and-flow and the rule that the question must
contain the variation. The sequence, the repetition unit, the coordination
structures beyond stock-and-flow, and the whole architecture are von Duyke.

Full attribution table: `nine_thinking_moves_attribution.md`.

## 4. The question form, and where cycles fail in the room

An aspect **is** a difference. A question that names a thing without naming a
difference cannot be answered, and the class goes quiet.

"How are polar bears adapted?" → silence.
"Which polar bear is more adapted, long fur or short?" → an answer, and then
"why did you choose?" opens it.

Narrow first, then widen. This inverts the common failure, where a teacher asks
open, meets silence, and collapses into gap-fill, at which point exploration is
over.

**Consequence for the decks:** if the Critical Aspect question names the aspect
and waits for the Contrast Set on the next slide to supply the difference, it is
structurally the polar bear question. This is mechanically checkable across all
26 decks and becomes a linter rule.

## 5. The coordination structures, correctly distinguished

Three objects of different kinds, which matters because it decides what can be
retrofitted.

**Stock-and-flow is a representation.** The student reads or builds a model; the
coordination is rate against amount. Independent of biology content. Can be
*added* to any cycle whose content accumulates.

**Compensatory pair is a case set.** Two real cases, same outcome, inverted
values on two aspects. *A sea turtle lays a hundred eggs and almost none survive;
an elephant produces one calf every four or five years and most survive; both
populations hold steady.* Must be *found* in the content.

**Conflict case is a single case** with opposing pulls. *Legless snakes: limbs
aid locomotion points one way, fitness is context-dependent points the other.*
Must be found.

**The what-if question is none of these.** A counterfactual requires the student
to hold the case, change an aspect, and generate the consequence, which she
cannot do without already holding the relation. It does not create coordination;
it reveals it. The first three are occasions; the what-if is evidence.

*Constraints on it:* specify what changes and what is held, or it becomes the
polar bear question. And in biology, hold the mechanism fixed and vary the
condition, because open counterfactuals invite teleological reasoning back in.

## 6. Visibility and complexity are separate scales

A hands-up vote asks for two things at once: a commitment and public exposure.
Where a wrong first answer is not yet safe, the exposure cost dominates and the
rational student looks at nobody.

**Visibility**, low to high: written and private → to a partner → read aloud
unattributed → owned aloud → voted.

**Complexity**, low to high: one aspect → a rule → a strained rule → two aspects
coordinated → a counterfactual.

They run on different clocks and should not be locked together. A vote is *low*
complexity at *high* visibility. A high-complexity question is usually easier in
a low-visibility form, because the student needs thinking time more than an
audience. If visibility rises monotonically across the arc, the highest-exposure
moves land on the hardest content, which is the worst pairing.

Raise visibility across the year. Reset it low within each cycle.

The two-tier response slide — written first answer, then rewrite — is the
low-visibility form of commitment, which is why it works in rooms where a vote
would produce silence.

### 6a. The ladder, gated by evidence

Rungs open on what the teacher can observe, not on week number. The mechanism
throughout is that the student is gathering evidence about what happens to wrong
answers, and she cannot get that evidence from being told.

1. **Written, seen only by the teacher.** Opens rung 2 when first answers are
   wrong in interesting ways rather than blank.
2. **Written, shown to one assigned partner.** Assigned, because chosen partners
   reproduce the social order. Opens rung 3 when students revise after talking.
3. **The teacher reads a wrong but productive answer aloud, unattributed, and
   uses it.** The class watches wrongness be valuable before anyone is identified
   with it. Opens rung 4 when students volunteer unsolicited.
4. **A student owns an answer aloud, by private invitation.** Opens rung 5 when
   disagreement happens between students rather than through the teacher.
5. **Public simultaneous commitment.** The vote. Last, not first.

Rung 3 is the one no professional development teaches and the one that does the
work. It resembles revoicing and resembles what Lampert does with student work;
the difference is that those treat it as a discourse move available when useful,
and this treats it as a scaffold with a position in a sequence and a precondition
for what follows.

### 6b. Visibility belongs to the move

The question sequence already ramps exposure, so mapping visibility onto the nine
moves gives the reset for free. Moves 1 and 2 tolerate the most, because a wrong
choice between named alternatives is cheap and productive. Move 4 tolerates the
least, because the student's own rule breaks there. This is also what tells a
teacher where cold call is safe.

[Rejected: a per-student readiness list. It is a written record that this student
cannot speak in front of the class, it follows her, and Cohen's status finding is
that expectations become self-confirming. Track by move instead.]

### 6c. Group work

A visibility reducer, not a rung. It permits high complexity at low exposure,
which is why the compensatory pair and conflict case belong there. But a group
product says nothing about who coordinated, so group work is a good occasion for
fusion and no evidence of it — which is why the what-if must be individual and
written.

### 6d. Assessment

A test is rung 5 without rung 3: maximum exposure, no evidence gathered about
what happens to a wrong answer, and the wrongness permanent and recorded. The
position is not to test a concept until students can coordinate the aspects
rather than recite them.

Where an artifact is required, build it from accumulated curriculum feedback rather
than administering an event. The reports already hold leveled evidence across a
cycle. The cost, stated honestly, is that this produces no common instrument and
cannot be compared across sections.

### 6e. The observation problem

A room at rung 1 or 2 shows quiet students writing, which reads as low engagement
against every walkthrough rubric in use, and is the correct instruction for that
room. Declaring the rung in the teacher note converts an absence into a decision,
and an observer can argue with a decision.

[This is the clearest institutional instance of the book's argument. A rubric
scoring visible participation without regard to what it costs a student rewards
teachers who front-load exposure and penalizes those building the conditions for
it — a motivation-model instrument applied to agency-model work.]

## 7. What the artifacts carry

**The teacher note.** One slide, not speaker notes, so it is visible to the
teacher and deletable in one action. Declares: the critical aspect, what is held
invariant, **what breaks if you substitute an example**, position in the
sequence, the slide-type map, which simultaneity the cycle works on, and the
visibility level.

Speaker notes are invisible to the audience this is written for, and a manual is
read after compliance is already the frame, if at all. A teacher adapting a deck
in good faith, not knowing three examples differ in exactly one dimension by
design, will substitute a better example and destroy the contrast. The note
protects the design against helpful improvement. In Marton's terms it protects the
intended object of learning as the enacted one changes hands.

**The Concept Bank.** Diachronic simultaneity as a built artifact. *Co-presence
is the precondition, not the achievement:* a page that lists the terms has
achieved co-presence and left the relating to chance. A page that asks the
student to state how two of them connect does the work.

**The fusion table.** One row per cycle across the arc: critical aspects,
coordination structure or none, reason for none, NGSS codes. This is what makes a
coverage gap visible, and what a teacher needs to plan across weeks. An absence
is not automatically a hole — content with no compensatory relationship and no
genuine conflict case is a design decision.

## 8. What this changes in feedback

Feedback runs through prompts shipped with the curriculum, not through an
operated pipeline. That is what makes this distributable: an adopting teacher can
run a prompt.

If a first submission and a rewrite are both scored on conceptual accuracy, the
same thing is measured twice and the rewrite becomes a correction exercise.

Two questions instead of one, and they belong in the prompt itself:

- Did the student discern the aspects?
- Did the student coordinate them when the case required both?

A student can be strong on the first and absent on the second, which under a
single accuracy score reads as partial understanding rather than as a specific
gap.

In whatever the prompt returns to the student, name what she discerned, then name
whether and how she brought two things together — in ordinary language, never in
the technical terms. In the revision prompt: a separation gap gets another
contrast; a coordination gap gets the case back with a question that will not
resolve on one aspect.

*Review sheets and cumulative tests attempt diachronic simultaneity and achieve
only co-presence, because filling a blank exercises retrieval and retrieval
leaves a sequence a sequence.*

## 9. Teacher judgment, and why it is the same construct

A student asks why snakes lost their legs. The teacher holds four things at once,
and resolves them in about two seconds:

- **The content.** Limb loss as derived, vestigial pelvic girdles, whether the
  student's framing implies evolution is goal-directed. Limb loss contradicts the
  improvement model the student carries. Resolving it takes two aspects held
  together.
- **His thinking.** He is holding a contradiction, not missing a fact, and he
  found it himself.
- **Him, in this room.** He is the boy who does not speak.
- **The design.** The cycle had an aspect under variation and somewhere to go,
  with twenty-five minutes left.

That is simultaneity in a teacher. The same construct, one level up, which is why
materials built to develop one can be built to develop the other.

**How judgment travels.** Procedures travel well; judgment does not. That is why
curriculum packages arrive intact and get enacted hollow. The mechanism that does
carry judgment is the **worked case** — not a principle stated abstractly, but a
record of a decision, including rejections. Teachers reconstruct the reasoning by
comparing cases, the same way students discern an aspect by comparing examples
that vary in one dimension.

A rationale document delivers one aspect at a time. A case is a moment where all
four are present and the reader must coordinate them to say what she would do.

**The design requirement:** the case must withhold the decision until the reader
has made her own. Otherwise she recognizes rather than judges, and recognition
does not transfer.

## 10. The package, and its central risk

Three parts, three readers:

1. **Agent-facing skill** — imperative, with triggers and checks, so another
   teacher's AI can execute it.
2. **Teacher-facing rationale** — why the structure exists, what it protects
   against, what breaks when removed. Lives with the artifact, not in a manual.
3. **The case file** — real decisions from real cycles, decisions withheld. The
   part no source material supplies.

**The risk, stated plainly:** a package that positions the adopting teacher as an
executor of the design is a motivation-model curriculum about an agency-model
pedagogy. The case file is what prevents that.

**The cost of taking that seriously:** a package that yields good lessons from a
teacher who never grasps the reasoning becomes a compromise rather than a
success. That is a slower and less marketable product than a curriculum house
wants.

**The disanalogy to name before a reviewer names it:** students are compelled to
be present and are being formed. Teachers chose the work and hold judgment being
developed rather than built. The version that treats them as adult learners with
existing expertise is the defensible one.

## 11. Position in the literature

**Variation Theory** (Marton; Marton and Booth; Marton and Tsui) supplies
separation and fusion and is largely silent on the teacher's real-time
coordination problem.

**Dialogic pedagogy** (Bakhtin on authoritative versus internally persuasive
discourse; Nystrand on authentic questions and uptake; Matusov) explains why a
student who never speaks speaks — a fact that resists assimilation cannot be
received as authoritative, so the only route through it is to talk. It declines
to specify the teacher's move.

**Lampert** (2001) is the nearest model for holding content, student, class, and
design at once, and leaves the coordination to the reader.

**Biology education research** supplies the evidence base. Nehm's work on
evolutionary reasoning documents that students handle gain-of-trait items
correctly and loss-of-trait items incorrectly. Legless snakes is a loss item,
which means the conflict case is aimed at a documented failure rather than a
general one.

**On visibility**, the adjacent work is Cohen's *Designing Groupwork* and complex
instruction, Tanner's participation structures, and accountable talk. None of it
treats exposure as a graduated dimension with its own clock, sequenced across an
arc, declared per cycle, and separable from cognitive demand. That is the gap,
and it has to be stated as a difference from those techniques rather than as
their absence.

**The contribution is parameters**: the layers to weigh, the terms that make a
vague judgment addressable, and the tradeoff made visible — without prescribing
the move. More structure than Lampert offers, less prescription than a dialogic
account permits.

*It will draw fire from both directions. Dialogic readers will call parameters
method creep; practitioner readers will ask why the move is not simply stated.
The test is not coherence but whether a parameter fires under time pressure with
thirty students, which argues for developing them against cases rather than
specifying them first.*

## 12. Build sequence

1. **Inventory run**, read-only, across 20 cycle folders. Everything below waits
   on it.
2. **Extend `deck_lint.py`** to two tiers. Hard failures block shipping; advisory
   items are reported. A linter that fails every deck gets ignored inside a week.
3. **Fusion retrofit**, one deck at a time, diagnosing and proposing rather than
   inserting. The case file is the byproduct of this run.
4. **Authoring skill**, public-facing, shipped with the rationale and the case
   file.

# The Nine Thinking Moves — Lineage and Attribution

Not nine questions. Nine moves, each with a cognitive purpose and a source.

Lineage: **Marton and Tsui** → **Moore-Anderson** → **von Duyke**.

## The traditions underneath

**Marton.** *Learning and Awareness* (Marton & Booth, 1997); *Classroom Discourse
and the Space of Learning* (Marton & Tsui, 2004); *Necessary Conditions of
Learning* (2015). Supplies the four patterns of variation — contrast,
generalization, separation, fusion — and the intended, enacted, and lived objects
of learning.

**Moore-Anderson.** *Teaching Meaning: What Works When Telling Isn't Enough*;
*Difference Maker*. Biology-specific, practitioner-authored, explicitly building
on Marton and Tsui. Supplies two things: the **question must contain the
variation**, and **stock-and-flow** as a question form for rate against amount.

[Citation note for the book: he is an independent practitioner-author, not
peer-reviewed literature. Cite him for design lineage, which is honest and
correct, but ground the underlying claims in Marton and Tsui so a reviewer has
something to check.]

**von Duyke.** Sequence, architecture, the coordination layer, and the
distribution of judgment.

## Move 1 — the flag

Moore-Anderson's argument is that an aspect **is** a difference, so a question
naming a thing without naming a difference cannot be answered. "How are polar
bears adapted?" produces silence. "Which polar bear is more adapted, long fur or
short?" produces an answer, and then "why did you choose?" opens it.

Your order is Critical Aspect question (slide 1), then Contrast Set (slide 2).

**The check:** does the Critical Aspect question itself carry a difference, or
does it name the aspect and wait for slide 2 to supply the contrast? If the
latter, it is structurally the polar bear question and the room will go quiet
before the contrast arrives.

[This is a linter rule and the inventory run can find it: does the move-1 slide
text contain a comparison, a choice between named alternatives, or a change
condition? Across 26 decks this is answerable mechanically.]

## The three coordination structures, correctly distinguished

I previously listed these as three fusion devices. They are not the same kind of
object, and the difference matters for the retrofit skill.

**Stock-and-flow is a representation.** The student reads or builds a model. The
coordination is rate against amount, which is a documented and stubborn
confusion independent of biology content — Booth Sweeney and Sterman's bathtub
studies found graduate students failing it. Source: Moore-Anderson.

**Compensatory pair is a case set.** Two real cases, same outcome, inverted
values on two aspects. Turtle and elephant. Nothing is modeled; the coordination
comes from the comparison. Source: von Duyke.

**Conflict case is a single case** with opposing pulls that cannot be resolved
from one aspect. Legless snakes. Source: von Duyke.

**The consequence for retrofitting:** a representation can be *added* to any
cycle whose content accumulates. A case set has to be *found* in the content and
cannot be manufactured. That is why the retrofit skill diagnoses rather than
inserts.

## The enactment protocol — currently missing

Moore-Anderson's piece supplies the classroom machinery that makes these
questions work in a room, and the decks do not carry it.

1. Establish the difference.
2. Multiple-choice question about the difference — narrow possibilities
   deliberately.
3. Pair talk.
4. Sequential hands-up vote, so the teacher sees the distribution.
5. Move chosen by the vote pattern: split vote → two students justify, then
   re-vote; low confidence → more pair talk, vote again; confident and wrong →
   several students justify, to surface what they perceived.
6. Widen: why did you choose?

This inverts the common failure. Teachers open, get silence, and close down into
gap-fill. Starting narrow makes the exploration possible.

## What is distinctly yours, stated plainly

1. **The fixed sequence, once per critical aspect.** Marton describes patterns of
   variation. He does not specify an order or a repetition unit. You made it a
   deck architecture.
2. **Coordination structures beyond stock-and-flow.** Compensatory pair and
   conflict case extend fusion into content that does not accumulate, which is
   most of biology.
3. **Diachronic simultaneity as a designed artifact.** The Concept Bank. Marton
   has the construct; nobody builds for it.
4. **The two-tier response structure.** Written first answer, then revision. The
   rule the student authored becomes the object the Pattern Break strains.
5. **The teacher note as a slide.** Design intent travelling with the artifact
   rather than in a manual.
6. **Separation and fusion as a teacher diagnostic**, replacing "she doesn't
   conceptualize it."
7. **The same construct at both levels** — the student coordinating aspects, the
   teacher coordinating content, student, class, and design.
